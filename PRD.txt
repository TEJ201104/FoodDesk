## Project Name
FoodDesk — Manage Smart. Serve Fast.

## Project Overview

Build a complete, production-quality, responsive web application using the MERN stack that digitizes the ordering, billing, payment tracking, and financial management operations of small food stalls and shops.

The primary objective is to eliminate traditional notebook-based order management and manual accounting.

The application should allow stall owners to create customer orders, manage food items, collect and verify payments, generate digital receipts, and automatically maintain financial records.

This is not merely a food delivery application. It is a Multi-stall Point of Sale (POS), order management, payment tracking, and business analytics platform designed to simplify daily operations for individual food stall owners.

## 6. POS and Order Creation

This is the most important feature.

Build a fast and intuitive Point of Sale interface.

### Order Placement Model

FoodDesk must follow an owner-assisted, walk-in ordering model.

- Customers must physically visit the stall and communicate their orders directly to the stall owner or staff.
- The stall owner or authorized staff member must enter all customer orders through the FoodDesk POS interface.
- Customers must not be able to create, submit, or place orders independently through their mobile phones or other devices.
- Do not implement customer-side shopping carts, online checkout, or self-service order placement.
- Customers may use their devices to complete payments, receive digital receipts, or check order status.

The interface should contain:

LEFT SECTION:
- Food item grid.
- Food images.
- Item names.
- Prices.
- Category filters.
- Search bar.
- Availability indicators.

RIGHT SECTION:
- Current order/cart.
- Selected items.
- Quantity controls.
- Remove item.
- Customer mobile number.
- Subtotal.
- Discounts, if applicable.
- Grand total.
- Payment button.

Order workflow:

1. Customer visits the stall.
2. Customer communicates their food selection to the stall owner or staff.
3. Stall owner or staff opens New Order in the POS.
4. Selects the requested food items.
5. Adjusts quantities.
6. Enters the customer's mobile number (optional).
7. System calculates the bill.
8. System creates an order with a unique order number.
9. Order enters Awaiting Payment status.
10. Customer completes payment.
11. Payment is verified.
12. Order becomes Confirmed.
13. Digital receipt is generated.
14. Customer receives the receipt.

### Daily Order Number Generation

Use unique order IDs such as:
ORD-YYYYMMDD-0001

Ensure order IDs are generated safely without race conditions.

Prevent duplicate order creation through idempotency mechanisms.

Reset the order sequence to 0001 at the start of each business day.

Order numbering must reset daily for each individual stall.

Use an atomic counter to prevent duplicate order numbers when multiple orders are created simultaneously.

The unique order number should be combined with the stall identifier internally to ensure uniqueness across the platform and enforce database-level uniqueness using the Stall ID and order number.

Store item names, selling prices, cost prices, and quantities as historical snapshots inside each order.

## 7. Payment Integration

Implement UPI payments.

Support integration with an appropriate payment gateway such as Razorpay or another provider that supports UPI payments.

The payment gateway should support applicable payment methods such as:
- UPI QR payments.
- UPI payments through applications such as Google Pay, PhonePe, and Paytm.
- NetBanking, where supported.
- Other payment methods supported by the selected provider.

Required functionality:
- Generate an order-specific payment request.
- Display the exact payable amount.
- Generate a payment QR code where supported.
- Allow customer to scan and pay using UPI applications.
- Verify payments on the backend.
- Use authenticated gateway webhooks.
- Validate transaction amounts and order references.
- Prevent duplicate payment processing.
- Store gateway transaction IDs.
- Maintain payment history.

IMPORTANT:
Never mark an order as paid based solely on a frontend callback, customer confirmation, or uploaded screenshot.

Only mark payments successful after trusted server-side verification.

For local development, implement a clearly labeled mock payment mode.

For the prototype, also provide a manual payment verification mode for static UPI QR payments. Store the transaction reference and verification details. Do not represent manual verification as automated gateway verification.

Support the following payment statuses:
- Pending.
- Processing.
- Successful.
- Failed.
- Refunded.

Support cash payments as an additional payment method.

### Manual and Cash Payment Verification

- For static UPI QR payments, the stall owner must manually verify the transaction against their actual payment account or transaction history.
- Store the UPI transaction reference, verification timestamp, and the stall owner's ID who verified the payment.
- Cash payments should be marked as successful only after the stall owner confirms receipt of the cash.
- Record who confirmed each manual or cash payment.
- Maintain an audit trail for all manual payment status changes.

### Payment Failure and Timeout Handling

- If payment fails, the order should remain in Awaiting Payment status.
- If payment remains pending beyond a configurable time limit, mark it as Payment Expired or require payment status re-verification.
- Allow the stall owner to retry payment or cancel eligible unpaid orders.
- Prevent duplicate successful payments from creating duplicate revenue entries.
- If payment succeeds after an order has been cancelled or expired, flag it for manual resolution or refund.

### Stall-wise Payment Configuration

- Each stall must have its own payment configuration.
- Store the stall owner's UPI ID and relevant payment details securely.
- Where supported, generate order-specific dynamic payment QR codes containing the exact payable amount.
- Ensure that payments are credited to the correct stall owner's payment account.
- Do not mix payment records or settlements between different stalls.
- If the platform uses a shared payment gateway, implement an appropriate merchant onboarding or marketplace settlement mechanism.
- For the prototype, allow stall owners to configure their own static UPI QR code.

## 11. Financial Analytics

Create an analytics dashboard.

### Financial Calculation Rules

Gross Sales = Total value of eligible sales before discounts and refunds.

Net Revenue = Gross Sales - Discounts - Refunds.

Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) = Total cost price of items sold, calculated using historical cost price snapshots and eligible order quantities.

Gross Profit = Net Revenue - COGS.

Net Profit = Gross Profit - Other Recorded Business Expenses.

Clearly distinguish:
- Gross sales.
- Discounts.
- Refunds.
- Net revenue.
- Cost of goods sold.
- Other expenses.
- Gross profit.
- Net profit.

Do not count pending or failed payments as collected revenue.

Do not count cancelled orders as sales.

Use historical price and cost snapshots.

Refunds must be deducted from revenue.

Discounts must be reflected in actual collected revenue.

Pending and failed payments must not count as collected sales.

Historical cost prices must remain unchanged.

Reports must be calculated independently for each stall.

Cash payments confirmed by the stall owner should be included in collected revenue, while pending cash payments must not be counted as collected sales.

Maintain separate reporting for cash payments and digital payments to support payment reconciliation.

Provide:
- Daily sales reports.
- Weekly sales reports.
- Monthly sales reports.
- Yearly sales reports.
- Custom date reports.
- Total orders.
- Average order value.
- Best-selling items.
- Least-selling items.
- Revenue charts.
- Expense charts.
- Profit/loss charts.

Allow CSV and PDF exports.

Use MongoDB aggregation pipelines for report calculations.

4. The system automatically calculates the bill.

6. A customer can make a payment using the configured payment method.
7. The backend verifies the payment.
8. The order is updated with the correct payment status.
9. A digital receipt is generated.

12. The owner can view previous orders.
13. The owner can record business expenses.
14. The dashboard displays accurate sales information.
15. The system calculates gross and net profit based on available cost and expense data.
16. Daily, weekly, monthly, and yearly reports work correctly.
17. Different stall owners cannot access each other's data.
18. The application works on desktop and mobile devices.
19. A stall owner can create and manage multiple authorized stall profiles under one account.
20. Newly created stall profiles remain pending until Super Admin approval.
21. Unapproved or inactive stalls cannot access POS and stall management features.

25. Stall owners cannot access another owner's business data.
26. Customers cannot independently place or submit orders through their devices. All orders must be created by the stall owner or authorized staff through the POS interface.

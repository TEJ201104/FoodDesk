1. FEATURE OVERVIEW
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This document is a beginner-friendly, plain-language reference guide designed for food stall owners, family operators, cashiers, and cooks who may have zero background in computer software, commercial accounting, or restaurant management systems.

Many food stall operators transition to FoodDesk directly from handwritten spiral notebooks and cash drawers. Technical terms like "P&L", "COGS", "Kanban", and "Multi-Tenant" can feel overwhelming. This guide translates commercial food service concepts into everyday, practical kitchen and counter language.

4. DETAILED PLAIN-LANGUAGE DEFINITIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

CATEGORY 1: FRONT-OF-HOUSE COUNTER & ORDERING TERMS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Point of Sale (POS):
  Your digital cash register. Instead of writing customer orders in a notebook with a ballpoint pen, the cashier taps pictures of dishes on a tablet or computer screen.
- Cart:
  The virtual tray on the right side of the POS screen that holds the food items a customer wants to buy before they pay.
- Pickup Token:
  The 3-digit number (like #101, #102) given to a customer so they know when their food is ready. When the kitchen finishes cooking, the cashier shouts: "Token 101 is ready!".
- Order Number:
  The official bill number (e.g., ORD-20261116-0001) printed on the receipt for tax, audit, and tracking records.
- Flat Discount vs. Percentage Discount:
  - Flat Discount: Taking a fixed rupee amount off the bill (e.g., "₹50 off for loyal customers").
  - Percentage Discount: Taking a percentage off the bill (e.g., "10% off for students").
- Change Due:
  The exact amount of physical cash cashiers must return to a customer when they pay with a larger currency note. (If the bill is ₹320 and customer gives a ₹500 note, Change Due is ₹180).
- Dynamic UPI QR:
  A special QR code on the screen that changes with every order. When scanned, it automatically fills in the exact bill amount on the customer's phone so they don't have to type it manually.

CATEGORY 2: BACK-OF-HOUSE KITCHEN & COOKING TERMS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Kitchen Display System (KDS):
  A TV or tablet screen in the kitchen that replaces physical paper order slips. Whenever the cashier enters an order at the counter, it instantly pops up on the kitchen screen with a bell sound.
- Kanban Board:
  A board divided into columns showing food moving through stages: Received → Preparing → Ready for Pickup → Completed.
- Special Instructions / Cooking Notes:
  Special customer requests entered at the counter (like "no onions", "extra spicy", or "less ice") that appear in a bright yellow warning box on the kitchen screen so cooks don't forget.
- Elapsed Timer:
  A live timer on every kitchen card showing how many minutes and seconds the customer has been waiting. If an order waits longer than 10 minutes, it turns bright red.

CATEGORY 3: INVENTORY & DAILY EXPENSE TERMS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Raw Materials / Ingredients:
  The basic food items you buy from wholesale markets to cook dishes (such as cooking oil, potatoes, chicken, onions, milk, tea leaves, and bread buns).
- Minimum Reorder Threshold:
  The safety alarm level for an ingredient. If you set potatoes to a minimum threshold of 5 kg, FoodDesk will show a warning banner whenever you have 5 kg or less remaining in stock.
- Wastage:
  Food that was purchased but could not be sold because it spoiled, expired, burned, or dropped on the floor. Logging wastage helps you find out why your kitchen is losing money.
- Operating Expenses:
  All the daily and monthly cash costs required to run your stall besides buying raw food (such as LPG commercial gas cylinder refills, electricity bills, staff wages, cleaning supplies, and paper bags).

CATEGORY 4: PROFIT, REVENUE & FINANCIAL ACCOUNTING TERMS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Gross Sales:
  The total face-value total of all food dishes ordered by customers before giving discounts.
- Net Revenue:
  The real money that actually entered your register till or bank account after deducting discounts given to customers and refunds returned to unhappy patrons.
- Cost of Goods Sold (COGS):
  The money you spent on raw ingredients to make the food that was actually sold. (If you sell a burger for ₹120, and the bun, patty, cheese, and sauce cost you ₹50 to buy, your COGS for that burger is ₹50).
- Gross Profit:
  What is left from your food sales after paying for the food ingredients. (₹120 selling price - ₹50 ingredient cost = ₹70 Gross Profit).
- True Net Profit:
  The real money the stall owner takes home in their pocket at the end of the day after paying for food ingredients AND all operating expenses (gas, electricity, rent, labor, and packaging).
  Formula: True Net Profit = Gross Profit - Operating Expenses.

11. FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS (FAQ)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Q1: If my Net Profit is negative, what does that mean?
A1: That means your stall operated at a Net Loss for that period: your total operating expenses (gas, rent, labor, packaging) and food costs were higher than the revenue collected from customers.

Q2: What is the fastest way to increase Net Profit?
A2: Either increase sales volume, reduce unrecorded ingredient wastage, negotiate wholesale discounts on high-volume raw materials (COGS), or eliminate slow-selling, low-margin dishes.
